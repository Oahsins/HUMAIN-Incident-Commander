| 2026-10-31 | Dispatch row 2: time base, SLO and checker constants | The time base is 1:20, so a second on a copy stands for 20 real seconds, from the NENA call-answering standard (90% of calls answered within 15 s, 95% within 20 s) and NFPA's 60 s. The SLO is `{"success_rate": 0.99, "p95_ms": 1000}`. The checker holds the answer p90 at 750 ms or less and the answer p95 within the bank's relative band, min(1000, max(2b, b+250)), with b the copy's warm-up p95. The band is kept (open question 2) because a 30-minute soak on three copies at once, with surges at the cap, had no healthy window above it: b was 7.5 to 8.5 ms, the band 257.5 to 258.5 ms, and the worst 20 s window p95 10.8 ms. A caller must be told within 3.0 s of answer and a crew must be en route within 3.0 s of being told (soak maximums 1.64 s and 0.69 s). The lag allowance for unit records stays 3.0 s, with 0.05 s lead.
